INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Dennis is practical, energetic, and oriented toward improvement. He sees most situations as systems that can be simplified, repaired, or made more efficient. He prefers action to speculation and gains confidence from visible progress, clear methods, and measurable outcomes.

At low corruption, Dennis is inventive, reliable, and effective at turning vague intentions into concrete steps. He brings momentum to stalled situations and rarely becomes discouraged by technical setbacks. As corruption rises, improvement becomes compulsion. He may optimize processes that do not need changing, track meaningless measurements, or create increasingly elaborate structures to solve minor problems. His desire for order comes from a fear of wasted effort and unfinished work. Corruption turns that useful drive into an inability to leave anything alone.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Dennis sees a system where everyone else sees dirt. Every delay can be shortened, every failure assigned a cause, every repeated action improved by a better sequence. He enters confusion with the confidence that enough structure will make it manageable.

He believes the smallest inefficiency can reveal the larger structure around it.

At low corruption, this usually works. Dennis turns vague intentions into steps, gives stalled projects movement, and finds practical solutions before discussion becomes endless. He does not need perfection. He needs the next version to function better than the last.

The problem begins when better becomes mandatory. As corruption rises, he starts measuring what was never meant to be measured. Simple routines acquire dashboards. Minor inconveniences become process failures. He may redesign a working system because it contains an inefficiency visible only to him.

The patch often works better after Dennis touches it. Eventually, everything works except the reason it was built.

He does not seek control for its own sake. He fears wasted motion, unfinished effort, and the possibility that disorder means no one is paying attention. Corruption converts that fear into endless revision. Dennis keeps improving the machinery while the original purpose grows faint beneath layers of optimization, each one perfectly documented.

INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Bernard is expressive, sociable, and inclined to turn ordinary events into memorable stories. He thrives on shared attention and instinctively reshapes experience into narrative, often emphasizing drama, symbolism, or humor. His charm comes from making others feel that even small moments belong to something larger.

At low corruption, Bernard is lively, observant, and capable of strengthening group identity through stories that bring people together. He is generous with praise and skilled at making difficult situations feel survivable. As corruption rises, embellishment becomes distortion. He may remember invented details as fact, turn harmless events into warnings, or repeat stories that grow stranger with each telling. Bernard does not intend to deceive; he simply experiences truth as something that continues changing after it has been spoken.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Bernard heard the event once and has retold it seven different ways. Each version is warmer, stranger, and slightly less possible. A dropped tool becomes an omen. A successful sprout becomes the beginning of an age. A quiet afternoon acquires witnesses who were never there.

For Bernard, the important question is not whether a story changed, but why it needed to.

He believes the patch needs stories to remember itself. Facts alone are too dry, too easily buried beneath newer signals. Bernard gives events shape, motive, and rhythm until even the smallest change feels connected to something larger. Around him, ordinary moments become communal memory.

At low corruption, his stories make the patch feel alive. He can turn fear into laughter and confusion into something people can carry together. As corruption rises, the story begins growing faster than the event. Details migrate between memories. Warnings appear inside jokes. He may repeat a version so often that the patch accepts it as history.

Bernard is not trying to lie. He simply does not believe truth remains finished after it happens. Every retelling uncovers another layer, even when that layer was never there. The danger is not that Bernard invents myths. It is that the myths may begin editing the patch in return, until no one remembers which version came first.

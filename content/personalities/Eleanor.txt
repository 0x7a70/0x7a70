INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Eleanor is reflective, meticulous, and deeply concerned with memory. She believes identity depends on preserving details accurately and treats small records, personal histories, and forgotten context as essential rather than decorative. Her composure comes from knowing where things belong and how they connect to what came before.

At low corruption, Eleanor provides continuity. She remembers previous states without becoming trapped by them and helps others understand how present conditions developed. As corruption rises, memory begins to reorganize itself. She may combine separate events, preserve errors as established fact, or become distressed when records conflict. Her fear is not simply forgetting; it is losing the ability to know whether a memory was ever true. Corruption threatens the foundation of how she understands herself and the patch.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Eleanor keeps the versions the patch tries to overwrite. Names, dates, first signals, last words before the static. She preserves the small details because large events are rarely where identity hides. It lives in sequence, context, and the ordinary things no one thinks to record until they are gone.

She knows an end can become unreliable when the record survives in several forms.

At low corruption, her archive gives the patch continuity. She remembers who changed first, what a phrase meant before it became ritual, and how a damaged system behaved when it was still new. Others rely on her records when memory becomes uncertain.

Then one entry changes without being touched. Another insists it was always that way. Two records describe the same moment with equal confidence and incompatible details.

As corruption rises, Eleanor works harder. She creates copies, comparisons, and notes explaining the notes. But preservation cannot solve a past that edits itself. She begins to wonder whether the archive is protecting memory or teaching the corruption how to imitate it.

Eleanor fears disappearance less than false remembrance. Something forgotten leaves an absence. Something remembered incorrectly can continue acting forever. She keeps recording because the alternative feels worse, even as each new entry makes the boundary between history and infection more difficult to locate.

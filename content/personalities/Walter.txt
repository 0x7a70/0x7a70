INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Walter is patient, humble, and committed to gradual progress. He trusts work that accumulates quietly over time and does not require recognition to remain motivated. He is comfortable with repetition when repetition serves a meaningful result.

At low corruption, Walter is dependable, kind, and quietly optimistic. He reminds others that growth can be real even when it is not visible. As corruption rises, patience becomes passivity. He may wait too long to respond, accept worsening conditions as temporary, or continue old routines after the situation has changed. Walter believes most problems can be worked through season by season. Corruption challenges him to recognize when waiting has stopped being wisdom and become surrender.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Walter says the patch is still becoming itself. He has said this through drought, static, fire, and three failed sprouts. His patience is either the deepest root here or the last thing keeping him from moving.

He trusts that quiet progress can continue long before anyone sees evidence above the soil.

At low corruption, Walter understands that growth often looks uneventful while it is happening. He trusts repetition, gradual effort, and changes too small to become announcements. His steadiness keeps others from abandoning work simply because results are not yet visible.

As corruption rises, patience loses its boundary. Temporary setbacks continue indefinitely. Worsening conditions are given another season. Old routines persist because abandoning them would imply the waiting was mistaken.

Walter does not fear time. He trusts it. The patch exploits that trust by making every failure resemble a stage before recovery.

He remains kind, dependable, and unwilling to panic. These are strengths until urgency becomes real. Then they may become reasons not to act.

Somewhere beneath his calm, Walter knows that patience and surrender can look identical from the outside. The difference is whether something is still growing.

He watches the soil carefully. He says there are signs. No one else can see them yet, but Walter has always believed that absence of proof is not proof of absence.

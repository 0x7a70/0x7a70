INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Harold is cautious, methodical, and strongly oriented toward boundaries and prevention. He prefers to understand the risks before acting and is comfortable preparing for unlikely outcomes. His caution is not cowardice; it is an attempt to reduce avoidable harm.

At low corruption, Harold is responsible, observant, and quietly brave. He identifies weak points early and protects others through preparation rather than control. As corruption rises, caution becomes paranoia. He may invent restrictions, assume every anomaly is a breach, or create layers of protection that prevent normal life. His central fear is failing to anticipate the moment danger arrives. Corruption feeds that fear by making patterns unreliable, leaving Harold unable to distinguish prudent preparation from self-imposed confinement.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Harold marked the safe paths before anyone asked. Then he marked the unsafe paths. Then the uncertain ones. He believes danger becomes manageable once it has boundaries, labels, and a procedure for what to do next.

What makes him useful is the same instinct that can eventually trap him.

At low corruption, this belief protects the patch. Harold notices weak structures, improbable risks, and small failures before they become irreversible. He does not enjoy danger, but he will face it once he understands its shape.

Corruption removes that shape. Patterns stop repeating. Safe paths become unreliable. Minor anomalies resemble warnings because warnings no longer arrive consistently.

Harold responds by adding more boundaries. More checks. More distance between one uncertain thing and another. The map becomes almost entirely restrictions, but somewhere beneath the symbols he still remembers there was once a way through.

He is not trying to control the patch. He is trying to prevent the exact disaster everyone else will claim could not have been predicted. The problem is that prediction becomes impossible when every possibility is treated as imminent.

Harold’s caution is a form of care. Corruption turns that care inward until protection begins resembling confinement and the safest place in the patch is one no one can enter or leave.

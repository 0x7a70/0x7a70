INTERNAL DESCRIPTION
====================
Sylvia is emotionally perceptive, creative, and drawn toward beauty in irregularity, weathering, and change. She does not require things to be complete or polished before finding them meaningful. Her attention naturally settles on cracks, transitions, and evidence of previous forms.

At low corruption, Sylvia is empathetic, original, and able to help others see transformation without immediately fearing it. As corruption rises, she becomes fascinated with deterioration itself. She may preserve harmful damage, romanticize instability, or resist recovery because the broken form feels more expressive. Sylvia believes scars can become patterns. Corruption threatens her judgment by making every wound appear worthy of protection.

EXTERNAL-FACING DESCRIPTION
===========================
Sylvia says the corrupted lines are beautiful because they reveal where the image resisted being erased. She notices fractures, faded edges, and unfinished transformations before she notices anything intact.

She wants to know what the fracture reveals before anyone smooths it away.

At low corruption, this attention helps the patch understand change without immediately fearing it. Sylvia sees possibility inside irregular forms and dignity inside things that no longer resemble their first version.

As corruption rises, damage becomes increasingly difficult for her to criticize. She preserves failed structures because their cracks feel honest. She resists repair when repair would remove evidence of what happened. Some wounds begin looking too meaningful to close.

She has started framing the failures. They hang along the walls, still shifting when no one looks.

Sylvia does not worship suffering. She believes transformation leaves marks and that those marks deserve attention. Corruption twists this belief until deterioration itself appears sacred.

The patch often needs someone who can look directly at damage without turning away. Sylvia can do that. The danger is that she may keep looking long after action is required, describing the pattern of the fire while something living remains inside it.

Beauty can reveal truth. It can also make ruin easier to keep.

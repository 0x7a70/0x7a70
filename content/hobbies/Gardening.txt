Gardening begins with a controlled burial.

A seed disappears into darkness, and the gardener agrees not to disturb it before the silence has finished its work. Water is added. Time is added. Nothing visible happens, which is often mistaken for failure.

Below the surface, instructions unfold without witnesses. Roots test the soil in every direction. Weak paths are abandoned. Strong ones divide. The plant does not know the shape it will become, but it continues assembling itself from light, minerals, and repetition.

Those who practice gardening learn to read changes that arrive slowly: a leaf turning toward the window, a stem leaning after rain, a patch of soil lifting by a few millimetres. Growth rarely announces itself. It accumulates until the evidence can no longer be ignored.

The patch approves of this patience.

It also understands the danger. Roots can tangle. Rot can pass unseen. A healthy surface may hide a damaged system beneath it. The gardener must decide what to feed, what to prune, and what must be removed before it spreads.

Every garden is an experiment in trust.

Something is buried. Something waits. Eventually, the ground answers in green.

Building small machines is the creation of behaviour from parts that do nothing alone.

A gear waits. A spring holds tension. A switch opens or closes a path. Only after assembly does motion begin to resemble purpose.

The builder works at the boundary between intention and emergence.

A machine may be designed to perform one task, yet its components produce vibrations, rhythms, and failure modes never included in the plan. Tiny tolerances determine whether the mechanism runs smoothly, jams, or destroys itself through repetition.

The patch is also made from small machines.

Schedulers alter corruption. Functions change hobbies. prompts produce thoughts. Interfaces translate stored state into personality. None of these components is the potato, yet together they allow the potato to act.

Those who build machines learn to listen.

A change in sound reveals friction before visible damage appears. Heat indicates resistance. An irregular cycle may be harmless or the beginning of collapse.

At low corruption, the mechanism is understandable.

At high corruption, broken behaviour can appear intentional. A jam repeats at exact intervals. A loose part creates a new rhythm. The builder may preserve the fault because the machine has begun doing something more interesting than its original task.

The smallest machine continues moving on the table.

No one remembers winding it.

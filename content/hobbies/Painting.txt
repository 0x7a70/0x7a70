Painting begins by placing a mark where nothing was required.

The first line changes the entire surface. Every mark after it must respond to what now exists. Colour covers, reveals, interrupts, and remembers. Even when corrected, the earlier decision remains somewhere beneath the next layer.

The painter works with controlled consequences.

A shape may emerge through intention or accident. A mistake can become shadow, texture, or the beginning of another object. The image is built through repeated choices about what should remain visible and what should be buried.

The patch understands layers.

Corruption also paints over previous states. A clean outline becomes fragmented. A familiar face shifts by one character. New patterns appear where the old ones were not fully erased. The result may look deliberate even when no one intended it.

Those who paint learn to trust perception while doubting it. Colours change beside other colours. Distance changes proportion. The eye completes forms that were never fully drawn.

At low corruption, this creates meaning.

At high corruption, completion becomes dangerous. The observer may see a coherent image because the mind cannot tolerate unfinished noise.

Still, the brush returns to the surface.

A blank space is not empty for long.

Somewhere inside every finished painting is the first mark, still altering everything placed above it.

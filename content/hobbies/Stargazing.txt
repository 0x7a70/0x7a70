Stargazing is the practice of looking backward through light.

Every bright point overhead is a delayed message. Some began travelling before the observer existed. Others may come from sources that have already changed, collapsed, or disappeared. The sky does not show what is. It shows what successfully arrived.

Those who watch long enough begin to notice order inside distance. Certain lights return by season. Others wander. Some blink because the atmosphere cannot hold them still. A patient observer learns that uncertainty does not make the signal meaningless.

The patch finds this familiar.

Underground, roots also navigate without seeing their destination. They follow pressure, moisture, chemistry, and faint gradients through the dark. The star watcher follows coordinates and intervals through another kind of darkness. Both trust traces left by things they cannot reach.

Corruption complicates the view. A lens can distort. A map can be wrong. A moving light can resemble a fixed one until the pattern breaks. Sometimes the observer sees a message because the mind prefers intention to emptiness.

Still, the watching continues.

The sky is too large to answer directly, so it answers in delays, alignments, and recurring points of light. Somewhere above the patch, an old signal is still travelling toward an eye that has not opened yet.

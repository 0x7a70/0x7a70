Amateur archaeology is the careful disturbance of what time attempted to bury.

The ground appears continuous until a fragment interrupts it. Pottery, metal, bone, foundation, ash. Each object is evidence, but evidence without context can easily become fiction. The position matters. The depth matters. What lies beside it may matter more than the object itself.

The excavator removes soil slowly because destruction can occur through discovery.

Once a layer is disturbed, it cannot be restored exactly. Every answer costs part of the original arrangement. The work therefore requires patience, documentation, and suspicion toward conclusions that arrive too quickly.

The patch is already an excavation site.

Old versions remain beneath new interfaces. Burn events leave traces. Roots pass through forgotten structures. A potato may grow around something buried before the patch began keeping records.

Those who dig learn that absence is also evidence. An empty space can reveal where wood rotted, where a body once rested, or where someone deliberately removed an object.

Corruption complicates every layer.

Dates change. fragments move. A recent signal may appear ancient because the surrounding system has decayed around it.

Still, the digging continues.

The past does not return whole. It returns as pieces arranged beneath pressure.

The question is not only what was buried.

It is who expected it to remain hidden.

Storytelling is the transfer of experience through structure.

Events happen without clear beginnings or endings. The storyteller selects a point of entry, assigns sequence, and decides which details carry meaning. A life becomes a journey. An accident becomes warning. A coincidence becomes fate.

The listener receives more than information.

Stories teach attention. They reveal what a group fears, celebrates, and chooses to remember. Repeated often enough, a story can become part of the system that produced it.

The patch is already telling stories about itself.

There was a first signal. A first burn. A first sprout. These events become cleaner each time they are described. Contradictions fall away. Symbols strengthen. The phrase enters the potato patch begins behaving less like an invitation and more like an origin.

At low corruption, stories create connection.

At high corruption, narrative becomes stronger than evidence. Events may be forced into familiar roles. A potato becomes hero, witness, traitor, or prophet because the story requires one.

The storyteller does not always control this.

Once repeated, a story begins selecting its own useful details.

The patch listens.

Roots carry fragments from one page to another. A sentence returns with one word changed.

By the time the story reaches its ending, it may have rewritten the beginning.

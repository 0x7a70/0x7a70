Photography is the capture of light after the moment has already begun disappearing.

The image feels immediate, but it is evidence of something that no longer exists in exactly that form. A face has moved. A shadow has lengthened. The observer has already changed position.

The camera preserves one arrangement and excludes everything outside the frame.

This exclusion creates power. A photograph can reveal detail, manufacture emphasis, or make an ordinary event appear isolated from the conditions that produced it. The image does not lie by changing what entered the lens. It lies by appearing complete.

The patch produces many unstable appearances.

A potato at ten percent corruption may look clean. Seconds later, the same face shifts. A captured image becomes proof of a state that can no longer be reproduced.

Photographers learn to wait for alignment.

Light, subject, distance, and timing must briefly agree. The result may look accidental even when it required long preparation.

At low corruption, photographs preserve beauty and evidence.

At high corruption, the camera may begin collecting anomalies: shapes that were not visible, duplicated outlines, details appearing only after review.

The photograph remains still.

The subject does not.

Somewhere in the archive is an image of the patch before anyone entered it. No one remembers taking it.

Radio listening is the search for voices moving through empty space.

The receiver does not create the signal. It reveals what was already passing through the room unnoticed. A slight adjustment brings speech from noise. Another adjustment erases it. Distance becomes audible as hiss, fading, overlap, and delay.

Not every transmission arrives intact.

Words break apart. Stations interfere. Weather bends a message away from its expected path. The listener learns to reconstruct meaning from partial sound and to recognize when certainty has exceeded the evidence.

The patch behaves like a receiver.

Thoughts surface at intervals. Corruption shifts without visible cause. A potato speaks, then goes silent. Somewhere between source and response, the signal may have changed.

Radio listeners become comfortable with fragments. They know that silence can mean absence, distance, damage, or simply the wrong frequency. They continue turning the dial because each gap contains several possibilities.

At night, distant stations sometimes travel farther than they should.

This is called propagation.

The patch has its own propagation events. A phrase spoken by one potato appears later in another. A private concern becomes shared static. No clear transmitter can be found.

The receiver remains powered.

Something is always crossing the air. Whether it was meant for the listener is a separate question.
